## 2. Kill criteria

> **Kill criterion:** If, by the end of Q1, sync is stable but fewer than 30% of site leads on low-signal pilot sites are logging 3+ days a week (up from 12%): - Offline work stops at its current scope. - 3 of the 5 mobile engineers move to Rock #2 (the simplified daily log), redirecting about $110k for the quarter. - The Q2 use cases are deferred by one quarter. This criterion tests assumption 1, which both revenue streams depend on. Comparing low-signal and good-signal sites separates “the offline feature works” from “the offline feature changes behaviour”. If logging rises only on good-signal sites, signal wasn’t the blocker. That would back up the WhatsApp evidence, and the money should go to simplifying the log instead.

## Lab notes

**Make your evaluation and funding decision**

- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The upsell rate rising from 6% to 8%. The whole case depends on a 2-point lift. If 8% is 25% too optimistic, the real rate is 6%, which is today’s baseline. At that rate the feature adds no upsells at all. The headline figure hides this: 32 upsells looks like a big number, but only 8 of them (2% of 400 accounts) would come from the feature.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The return counts upsells that would have happened anyway. 24 of the 32 upsells (6% of 400) already happen today. The extra return is really 8 × $28k = $224k, not $896k. Payback goes from 2.4 months to about 9.6 months ($180k ÷ $18.7k a month). It’s also generous in two more ways: - It counts the full $28k Enterprise contract as new revenue. It doesn’t subtract the Standard fee these customers already pay. - It ignores the 12% churn and the two-quarter ramp. There’s also a problem with the logic. The feature is built into the Standard tier, so it gives customers less reason to upgrade, not more. Nothing explains how a 20% faster time-to-quote leads to an Enterprise upgrade.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** Partly. It names a consequence (pause the feature and move Q4 capacity elsewhere), so it doesn’t just hand the decision back to the room. But the threshold is too low: - At 7%, the feature adds about 4 upsells, or $112k ARR. That’s roughly a 19-month payback, yet the feature still passes. - It measures total upsell, not upsell caused by the feature, so normal variation could trigger it or save it. On 400 accounts, 1 point is just 4 deals. - “End of Q3” isn’t tied to the launch date, so the measurement window is unclear.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** Rewrite the kill criterion so it measures the extra upsell from the feature: accounts using the feature compared against the 6% baseline. Set the bar at ≥7.5% by end of Q3. That’s about 6.4 extra upsells, which pays back the $180k within 12 months. Below that, the pause and reallocation happen automatically. The case doesn’t give the Standard price. Until it does, the gain per upsell and the kill threshold are both overstated. The 7.5% figure is a minimum, and it goes up once the Standard price is netted off.
